## Purpose & contribution claims
- Examines how senior marketing leaders of multinationals respond to the convergence of generative AI, geopolitical volatility, and regulatory complexity (p. 34).
- Conceptual: synthesizes fragmented debates (AI ethics, global branding, organizational agility) into one framework capturing the paradoxes **automation vs authenticity, speed vs trust, personalization vs privacy** (pp. 34–35, citing Grewal et al. 2025a).
- Theoretical: extends **institutional turbulence theory** (Greenwood et al. 2011); reframes the **CMO as cross-functional integrator** of data, culture, governance (Moorman & Day 2016); mobilization of **dynamic capabilities** (Teece 2007; Teece, Peteraf & Leih 2016) under uncertainty.

## Method (pp. 35)
- **Practitioner-centered qualitative design**: ONE recorded executive panel at the AMA Global Marketing SIG Conference, Sydney, May 2025.
- Panelists (n=3, footnote 1): Nicolas Chu (CEO, Sinorbis), Wendy Mak (CMO, MUFG), Mark Phibbs (CMO, Movius; ex-Cisco/Adobe/Microsoft/IBM).
- Audio recorded → AI-based automatic transcription → manual check/correction/anonymization (Tolle et al. 2024).
- Analysis: inductive, **reflexive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke 2006) + Gioia methodology (Gioia, Corley & Hamilton 2013)**; open line-by-line coding → first-order categories → second-order themes → aggregate dimensions; abductive movement between data and literature; purposive information-rich sampling (depth not breadth, Palinkas et al. 2015).

## Findings structure
**Figure 1** (p. 36): data structure — **3 clusters** (organizational transformation; technological governance; cross-market strategy) → **7 strategic imperatives**: role of the CMO; generative AI integration; organizational agility; ethical AI governance; data infrastructure barriers; brand architecture evolution; global–local strategy tension.
**Table 1** (p. 37): per-imperative key insight + representative quote (e.g., "The modern CMO is no longer just a brand custodian…"; quarterly strategy/budget redo; privacy as the biggest showstopper; unstructured data across thousands of clients; branded house across 16 jurisdictions; "act locally but think globally… pendulum always swings").

### Per-imperative highlights (pp. 36–43)
1. **Role of the CMO** (pp. 37–38): CMO as tech strategist/policy analyst/culture translator/risk manager; expanded C-suite (CDO, chief ethics officers); executive storytelling as strategic enabler ("change translators", Pedowitz/Forbes 2024; McKinsey CMO "comeback", Bettati et al. 2025); tensions short-term efficiency vs long-term brand, automation vs human creativity (Grewal et al. 2025b).
2. **Generative AI integration** (pp. 38–39): Coca-Cola, Heinz, L'Oréal, IKEA mainstreaming AI; **Figure 2** (p. 38): Cisco's AI-enabled customer support workflow — hybrid intelligence, automation **82% of inquiries** with human oversight; **Figure 3** (p. 39): IKEA Southeast Asia campaign — AI-augmented consumer data + localized storytelling **doubled engagement** vs global-standard content; risks: brand commodification, content homogeneity, cultural appropriateness — train AI on sociocultural cues, not just language.
3. **Organizational agility** (pp. 39–41): planning annual → quarterly; institutional turbulence (Malik & Terzidis 2025); improvisational capability (Akgün et al. 2007); Cisco "The Hatch" in-house agency → bottlenecks → "mini-hatcheries" in Asia/Europe (decentralized-but-aligned); dynamic capabilities (Cavusgil & Deligonul 2025; Asghar et al. 2025); agile-at-scale enablers (Rigby et al. 2018): cross-functional squads, iterative planning, psychological safety; SEA/LatAm pilots (livestream shopping Vietnam, mobile-first loyalty Brazil); new hybrid roles (prompt engineers, citizen data scientists, AI ethicists, data translators); internal resistance → reskilling/change management (Brock & Wangenheim 2019).
4. **Ethical AI governance** (pp. 41–42): ethical pluralism across markets; >80 disparate AI ethics guidelines (Jobin et al. 2019); EU AI Act, US EO 14110, China algorithm regulation; adaptive governance anchored in fairness/accountability/transparency; bias-detection, explainable AI, human-in-the-loop; disclosure of AI involvement (finance/health); Booz Allen "vertical scaling" context-aware governance (Wilkers 2024); ethical governance as brand-equity dimension.
5. **Data infrastructure barriers** (pp. 41–42): legacy IT, inconsistent taxonomies, jurisdictional fragmentation; unified data architectures foundational (Manyika et al. 2017); omni-brand orientation barrier (Schmidt-Devlin, Özsomer & Newmeyer 2022); federated systems; master data management, data lakehouses, chief data officers; synthetic data for sparsity/privacy.
6. **Brand architecture evolution** (p. 42): Mak's house-of-brands → branded house across 16 jurisdictions; Keller (2014) brand architecture & equity; Douglas & Craig (2011) semiglobal; AI-enabled brand management (Chatterjee et al. 2021); cultural alignment + internal storytelling; change management process.
7. **Global–local strategy tension** (pp. 42–43): "real-time glocalization" via AI (localized content generators, cultural sentiment analysis); WeChat/WhatsApp platform localization — WhatsApp India +30% engagement; structures of common difference (Schau, Akaka & Segabinazzi 2023); SEA A/B test: local lifestyle visuals +40% conversion; translation → **transcreation**; context-aware AI trained on regionally diverse datasets.

### Implications (pp. 43–44)
CMO oversight within tech/analytics governance; agility via decentralized-empowered creative structures; AI reconciles global identity vs local relevance (Pitelis, Teece & Yang 2023 — AI modifies sensing/seizing); "rogue automation" risk to brand equity/trust; AI dashboards for real-time crisis management (COVID-19, Russia–Ukraine war); over-prioritizing algorithmic metrics erodes narrative coherence/brand meaning (Keller 2014); sociocultural intelligence in algorithmic design.

### Future research (p. 44) — **Table 2** (p. 40): 5 strategic priorities → 16 RQs → H1–H16
(1) AI-driven strategy & org design (H1–H4); (2) global consistency vs local relevance (H5–H7, incl. branded house vs house of brands, AI authenticity × individualism-collectivism); (3) ethical governance & trust (H8–H10, transparency cues); (4) geopolitical & market uncertainty (H11–H13, short-termism vs brand equity); (5) human–AI collaboration & workforce (H14–H16, replacement vs augmentation, uncertainty-avoidance cultures).

### Conclusion (p. 45)
Polarized future: displacement/weaponization/destabilization vs augmentation/efficiency/societal gains; need for theory & empirics on trust, governance, cross-cultural AI adoption, human–AI value exchange.

## Critique hooks (for §Critical Appraisal)
1. **Evidence base = one panel, three executives** (tech-leaning, Australia-hosted): purposive depth claimed, but generalizability of "global marketing leadership" claims from n=3 is thin; no member checking reported; panel format (public conference) invites curated, reputationally safe narratives vs candid data.
2. **Method-label stacking** (reflexive TA + Gioia + abduction) is rigorous-sounding but the audit trail (codebook, first-order category lists) is not shown beyond Figure 1; AI-transcription bias acknowledged only via manual checking.
3. **Insight Paper genre**: hypotheses (H1–H16) are generated, not tested — the article's real contribution is agenda-setting; treat Table 2 as its strongest scholarly artifact.
4. **Strengths:** highly current (EU AI Act 2024, EO 14110); vivid mechanism-level cases (Cisco 82% automation + mini-hatcheries; IKEA SEA 2× engagement; WhatsApp India +30%; SEA A/B +40%); careful triangulation with a broad, recent literature; honest acknowledgment of tensions/paradoxes; clear managerial relevance.
5. **Numbers are practitioner-claimed**, not independently verified (82%, 2×, 30%, 40%) — rhetorical force vs evidentiary status.
