**Citation:** Kostruba, A., & Kostruba, O. (2025). Strategies for Companies to Enter International Markets: Literature Review and Conceptualization. *Scientific Annals of the Danube Delta Institute (DANUBE)*, 16(2), 154–175. **Authors' affiliation:** Université Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne.
**Source file:** `Article/Artikel 13.pdf` (23 PDF pages; journal pp. 154–175). Page map: journal p. = PDF p. + 152 (PDF 2 = p. 154).

## Purpose & research aim
- Systematize/conceptualize current approaches to building strategies for companies entering international markets, **specifically adapted to the Ukrainian context** (war economy, falling domestic demand, SME resource constraints) (pp. 154–156).
- Empirical/applied anchor: first-time applied study of the **coffee markets of Germany and Poland** as target markets for Ukrainian (FMCG) exporters (pp. 155–156, 160–162, 171–173).
- Claimed novelty: (1) revised conceptual model of the international marketing environment adding **institutional maturity of the exporting country, social capital (diaspora), and support-infrastructure availability**; (2) attention to modern "easy-entry" modes — marketplaces, crowdsourcing/crowdfunding, pilot sales, diaspora participation (pp. 154, 164, 169, 171–172).

## Type & method
- **Evaluative literature review + conceptualization** (review and inductive research). NOT an empirical hypothesis-testing study.
- Literature selection (p. 156): relevance ≤10 years; Ukrainian + foreign authors; searched via **Google and Google Scholar**; exclusion: >10 years old, no UA/EN version, paywalled. Keywords listed.
- Methods narrative (pp. 159–160): monitoring (collecting/structuring empirical data: statistics, expert opinions, surveys, open databases); comparative technique; analysis & synthesis; induction; abstraction/abstract-logical/dialectical methods; concretization; "reasoning technique." (Methodologically loose — critique target.)

## Structure & key content

### Table 1 (pp. 157–158): comparative approaches to entry strategies
7 author groups compared (Loshenyuk/Galan/Posokhov; Kovtun/Denisevych/Zaitseva; Kovbatiuk/Shkliar/Kovbatiuk; Khryniuk & Ganich; Katsikeas/Leonidou/Zeriti; Paul; Griffin & Pustay; Hill) — object, key aspects, methodology, findings.

### Results (pp. 160–169)
- **Coffee-market macro-environment factors** (legislation stability; taxation; state quality control) → **Table 2** (p. 161): Germany vs Poland: German FDI law equal treatment, strong legal tradition; Poland Civil/Commercial Code, foreigners cannot buy land, CPI rank 30/180 (2015); German tariffs: green coffee 0%, roasted 7.5%, VAT 7%, **coffee tax €2.19/kg roasted, €4.78/kg instant**; Poland VAT 23%; EU Common Food Law + single food-safety agency.
  - Strategic inference (p. 162): invest in green-coffee processing in-market to avoid roasted-coffee duty/tax; use as brand attribute. (NOTE: text says "Based on Table 1" — labeling slip; it means Table 2. Critique candidate.)
- **Figure 1** (p. 162): main motives for entry — driven by small/imperfect domestic market; decision only after domestic-market analysis. (Source: Dado et al. 2015; Fedorenko 2020.)
- **Figure 2** (p. 163): interconnection of markets with the identity **F ∩ N = G** (foreign ∩ national = global). (CRITIQUE: as set theory this is dubious — intersection vs union confusion; text says "combination (unification)" while formula uses ∩. Major formal weakness.)
- **Figure 3** (p. 164): components of the international marketing environment; political-legal component → three legal systems (common law, civil law, theocratic law) with business implications (pork-in-Iran example).
- Proposed **revised model**: internal readiness + external (political, legal, economic, socio-cultural) + NEW: institutional maturity of exporting country, adaptive low-risk strategy capability, access to incubators/infrastructure/finance/expertise (p. 164).
- Socio-cultural environment (pp. 165–166): language, social organization, legal culture, political culture, science/education, religion/values; **Hofstede dimensions** (power distance, uncertainty avoidance, individualism-collectivism, masculinity-femininity) with demand-prediction examples.
- Economic environment → **Figure 4** (p. 166): components of the economic environment of international marketing (based on Daniels et al. 2019; Belch & Belch 2018).
- Foreign-market research goals (p. 166–167): feasibility, attractiveness, purchasing power, sales system, competitive segments; team-of-analysts.
- **Internationalization methods, three categories** (pp. 166–168): exports (direct / indirect / joint — knowledge & control trade-offs), joint ventures (JV, licensing, franchising — trust/legal protection/interest alignment; fits medium firms), direct investment (most control/image/long-term but most costly; fits large firms).
- **Table 3** (p. 167): three strategy groups — trading (produce at home, export), cooperative (transfer production via agreement with local company), hierarchical (production abroad by majority-owned international company). Active vs passive internationalization.
- **Table 4** (p. 168): joint business activity types — licensing, contract manufacturing, contract management, jointly owned enterprises.
- Direct investment advantages (p. 168): control over finances; reliable relations with all consumption levels; lower transport costs; image + jobs.
- **Size-contingency prescription** (p. 168): small firms → export (indirect/joint if no language/market skills); medium → exports & JV; large → any, direct investment for priority markets.
- **Adaptive Ukrainian strategy** (p. 169): progressive internationalization with risk reduction & learning; pilot sales, crowdsourcing, market cooperation; institutional assistance (incubators, export centers, digital platforms); diaspora as social capital.

### Discussion & recommendations (pp. 169–171)
8-step entry checklist (goals; product SWOT-style study; resource/profit pre-calculations; target-country specifics; peer-company experience; value-resource fit; low-resource entry method first then scale; integrate marketing/managerial innovations). Five Ukraine-specific proposals: sectoral export centers in the EU; gradual "easy entry" models (marketplaces, temporary licensing, crowdfunding); diaspora cooperation (ethnomarketing); public digital platform of entry case data; EU-certification adoption scheme with state cost reimbursement.

### Conclusion (pp. 171–172)
Systematization + revised environment model + adaptive phased-entry model; coffee case as demonstration model extendable to other FMCG.

## Critique hooks (for §Critical Appraisal)
1. **Method opacity:** "Google and Google Scholar" as the search engines; no PRISMA-style protocol, no source counts, no inclusion flow; "monitoring method" and "reasoning technique" are vague labels. Generalizability claims exceed the method.
2. **F∩N=G formula is formally incoherent** (intersection presented as "unification"); Figure 2's conceptual value vs mathematical dressing.
3. **Labeling slip** ("Based on Table 1" when discussing Table 2 content, p. 162) and dated evidence (2015 CPI; some claims rest on news items).
4. **Conceptual-model novelty is asserted, not tested** — institutional maturity/social capital/diaspora variables are plausible but no operationalization or validation; recommendations are policy-like, not firm-level tested.
5. **Strengths:** clear size-contingent prescriptions; genuinely useful Germany-vs-Poland fiscal detail (coffee tax asymmetry → green-coffee processing arbitrage); timely Ukraine/war framing; integrates Hofstede + legal-systems lens; rich UA + international source mix.
6. **Ch.7 linkage:** maps cleanly onto TPGS §1 (motives ↔ Figure 1), §3 (five entry options ↔ exports/JV/licensing/franchising/subsidiary–direct investment; Tables 3–4), §2c/2e (host-government policy & socio-cultural differences ↔ Table 2, Figure 3, Hofstede), §7–8 (developing-economy perspective — but INVERTED: TPGS views entry INTO emerging markets / defense BY local firms, Kostruba² views emerging-economy firms entering DEVELOPED markets; productive contrast). Diamond model absent from the article — TPGS Figure 7.1 can be offered as the missing home-country-advantage lens (Ukraine's factor conditions for agri-food/coffee processing).
